## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the canonical identity and conceptual foundation for the Atlas page in NeuralVerse. It defines what Atlas *is* at the highest level of abstraction, before any implementation details are considered.

## 2. Atlas Identity

**Atlas = Knowledge Topology**

Atlas is the spatial representation of how AI Engineering knowledge connects. It is not a graph, not a map, not an observatory — it is the *topology* of knowledge relationships.

### 2.1 Why Topology?

Topology captures the essential structure of knowledge connections without prescribing specific visual representations. It focuses on:

- **Connectivity** — which concepts are related
- **Proximity** — how closely concepts are related
- **Hierarchy** — which concepts subsume others
- **Flow** — how knowledge dependencies propagate

### 2.2 What Atlas Is Not

Atlas is explicitly NOT:

- A **graph** — that's the computational implementation layer (NV-700-M3)
- A **map** — that implies geographic metaphor, which doesn't fit knowledge
- An **observatory** — that implies passive observation, not active exploration
- A **browser** — that implies hierarchical list navigation
- A **curriculum** — that's the Learning system's responsibility

## 4. Core Concept

### 4.1 Knowledge Topology Defined

Knowledge Topology is the study of:
- How knowledge entities connect
- What patterns those connections form
- How those patterns evolve over time
- What implications those patterns have for learning and application

### 4.2 Atlas's Role

Atlas provides the *spatial interface* to this topology. Users can:

1. **See** the relational structure of AI knowledge
2. **Navigate** between connected concepts
3. **Filter** by relationship type or domain
4. **Discover** emergent patterns and clusters
5. **Understand** how concepts depend on each other

## 5. Differentiation from Other Systems

### 5.1 Atlas vs Learning

- **Learning** = sequential progression through curriculum
- **Atlas** = spatial exploration of knowledge relationships

### 5.2 Atlas vs Modules

- **Modules** = organized collections of related content
- **Atlas** = the connections *between* modules and their concepts

### 5.3 Atlas vs Workspace

- **Workspace** = hands-on experimentation environment
- **Atlas** = understanding how experiments relate to theory

### 5.4 Atlas vs Content

- **Content** = individual knowledge artifacts
- **Atlas** = the relational structure connecting those artifacts

### 5.5 Atlas vs Retrieval

- **Retrieval** = finding specific knowledge on demand
- **Atlas** = understanding the landscape of available knowledge

### 5.6 Atlas vs Visualize

- **Visualize** = parametric visualization of individual concepts
- **Atlas** = relational visualization of concept connections

## 6. Design Principles

1. **Relational Primacy** — Every element represents a relationship, not an entity
2. **Spatial Coherence** — Related concepts appear near each other
3. **Progressive Disclosure** — Complexity reveals itself gradually
4. **Multiple Perspectives** — Same topology, different views
5. **Semantic Fidelity** — Visual distance reflects conceptual distance
